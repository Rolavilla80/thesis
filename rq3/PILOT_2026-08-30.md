## Reading
The paper's constraint delivers exactly what it promises (no mixed full+partial byte tokens; the
117 leftovers are incomplete characters). One level up, about 1,400 of 64,000 tokens are grapheme
fragments in Thai, and the authors' `_cbi` option would catch 2 of them, because Thai marks have
Script=Thai, not Inherited. The proposed `_cbg` rule (DESIGN_grapheme_constraint.md) targets these.
Note for the rule: L1 must look at the first non-space character (333 cases start with a space).

## JUNK ANATOMY (2026-09-03, rq3/junk_anatomy.py on the unconstrained bytes tokenizers; Windows venv reading the WSL files)

Question (from census finding 6): is non-prefix junk really the frame-shift cascade, i.e.
does every junk token match [continuation-byte run] + k complete chars + [dangling
lead-byte prefix], with at least one bad edge? Four counts over ALL junk tokens, not
just the 8 printed examples.

| | heb | arb | tha |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-prefix junk tokens | 15,490 | 59 | 42,759 |
| MATCHES CASCADE SHAPE | **15,490 (100.00%)** | **59 (100.00%)** | **42,759 (100.00%)** |
| one bad edge / two | 14,794 / 696 | 57 / 2 | 26,498 / 16,261 |
| ends in dangling lead prefix | 6,807 (43.9%) -- 6,760 are exactly 'd7' | 51 (86%) | 29,283 (68.5%) -- 29,248 are exactly 'e0 b8' |
| starts with continuation byte | 9,379 (60.6%) | 10 (17%) | 29,737 (69.6%) |

Findings (all with examples):
1. CASCADE CONFIRMED, ZERO exceptions in 58,308 junk tokens across the three scripts.
   Census finding 6's "strongly supported" is upgraded to CLOSED. The general law:
   unconstrained junk = a character-grid span misaligned at exactly one or two edges,
   at whatever byte width the script exposes (2-byte Hebrew, 3-byte Thai).
2. HEBREW'S JUNK SEED IS MERGE #1: token id 257 (the first merge of the whole run!) is
   '20 d7' = space + the shared lead byte of every Hebrew letter. The most frequent byte
   pair in the Hebrew corpus is already malformed; the cascade grows from there
   ('d7 95 d7' = vav + dangling d7 at id 260, '94 d7' = tail-of-he + dangling d7 at 262).
   99.3% of Hebrew's dangling tails are exactly 'd7' (6,760/6,807); the rest: 'd6' 19
   (niqqud range), stray 'c2'/'d8'/'d0'.
3. THAI: 99.9% of dangling tails are exactly 'e0 b8' (29,248/29,283); two-bad-edge junk is
   common (38% vs Hebrew's 4.5%) because a 3-byte char offers two cut positions (e.g.
   id 266 '81 e0 b8' = tail of ko kai + prefix of the next char); length mod 3 = 0
   dominates (22,811 of 42,759), the frame-shift rhythm.
4. ARABIC: the cascade exists but stays subcritical: 'd8 a7 d8' (alef + dangling d8) was
   merged EARLY (id 266), yet Arabic-native junk types stay in the dozens; a good share
   of its 59 junk is foreign-script debris ('20 c2', '20 e2 80', tails 'ef bb', 'd0',
   even 'd7'). Consistent with lead-byte dilution across D8/D9: each shifted pair's
   frequency lands under the merge cutoff (~last-merge freq 58) instead of thousands over.
5. Thesis sentence this licenses: "In every language examined, 100% of unconstrained junk
   tokens are frame-shifted character spans; the cascade ignites exactly where an
   alphabet's UTF-8 bytes concentrate on a single lead byte (Hebrew D7, Thai E0 B8) and
   stays subcritical where they are diluted (Arabic D8/D9)."

### Junk-anatomy discussion outcomes (2026-09-03, R. + friend + Claude)

1. TERMINOLOGY ADOPTED for the thesis: "frame-shifted / boundary-misaligned tokens"
   (e.g. '81 e0 b8' = one character's worth of bytes in the wrong phase; '94 d7' = letter
   tail + next letter's lead). "Mixed junk" only as informal shorthand. The scientific
   point is precisely that they are NOT random bytes: 100% are valid UTF-8 internally,
   misaligned only at the edges.
2. PRECISION on what 100% shape-match proves: all junk is frame-shift-COMPATIBLE
   (anatomy) and the malformed units appear among the very first merges (Hebrew ids 257,
   260-266; Thai 261-275) (chronology). It does NOT yet prove descent ("one early bad
   merge breeds thousands") — that needs merge ancestry.
3. Seeds vs population: the cascade SEEDS are identical two-bad-edge pure shifted units
   in both scripts ('94 d7' = cont[1]+prefix[1]; '81 e0 b8' = cont[1]+prefix[2]), but the
   grown populations differ: Hebrew 95.5% one-bad-edge (valid span + one broken boundary,
   odd lengths 2k+1), Thai 38% two-bad-edge (deeply phase-shifted, mod-3 rhythm). Thai is
   not "Hebrew but larger"; it is more deeply misaligned.
4. Hebrew-vs-Arabic cause stays "supported, not proven": D7 concentration vs D8/D9

### The Hindi catastrophe — and its mechanism (the finding of RQ3.3)
111.53/1000 (14x worse than _cb), 24.85% of all boundaries illegal, 9,553 of 9,589
violations virama-adjacent (प|्र 526x, क|्ष 104x), +29.5% more tokens on identical

unassemblable by greedy BPE:
  a conjunct क्ष = क + ् + ष needs two merges, so it must pass through one of two
  intermediate states: [क्] (blocked by R2: ends in virama) or [्ष] (blocked by L1:
  starts with Extend). Both scaffolding states banned => the full cluster is
  UNREACHABLE, and every occurrence of every conjunct is permanently split at the
  virama. Under _cb, the census's 924 virama-final and part of the 2,860 mark-start
  "fragment" types were LOAD-BEARING SCAFFOLDING for assembling complete clusters.
The preregistered caveat ("projections assume merge paths don't shift qualitatively")
turned out to be the entire story for Hindi. Type-level rules INTERACT: L1 alone is
safe for Thai/Punjabi/Arabic (marks attach in one merge, base+mark is always legal);
L1+R2 is lethal exactly where clusters span 3+ codepoints.
Thai top cuts now include corpus-typo clusters ('ทีี่' doubled vowel) — residue partly
data noise, worth a footnote.

## Generalization of _cbg to the other 8 languages + naming gloss (2026-09-04 discussion)

1. WHERE _cbg HELPS AND WHERE IT IS A NO-OP: it fixes cuts involving dependent marks
   in RUNNING TEXT, so it helps exactly tha/hin/pan/arb (done above). For deu/eng/vie/
   rus it has nothing to do (residue 0.00-0.03/1,000 under _cb). For kor/jpn/zho the
   residue (0.26-1.04) is entirely CHARACTER-INTERNAL (rare chars never assembled,
   e.g. '濤', '솅') — that is the byte-assembly layer, untouched by _cbg by design.
   => Thesis formulation: "harmless everywhere, valuable where it matters" — the
   property that justifies _cbg as a global default.
2. HEBREW IS DOMAIN-DEPENDENT: _cbg would remove its 377 niqqud fragment TYPES
   (e.g. 'ָה'), but on FLORES (modern, unpointed) the usage benefit is ~zero
   (0.03/1,000). On POINTED text (liturgical, children's books, poetry) those types
   fire and _cbg would matter. Connects RQ3.3 to RQ3.4's cross-domain question: the
   constraint's value depends on the text domain, and Hebrew is the language where
   the domain flips the answer. Same for emoji rules R1/R3/R5: silent on news text,
   relevant on social-media-like text.
3. NAMING GLOSS for the record: "virama" = Devanagari ् (U+094D, halant), the
   VOWEL-KILLER: क="ka"; क+् = bare "k", which welds to the next consonant into a
   conjunct (क+्+ष = क्ष "ksa"). R2 was the rule "no token may END in a virama".
   `bytes_gpt4o_cbg_novirama` = bytes + gpt4o regex + grapheme constraint + NO-virama-
   rule (R2 off) — the variant that became the recommended default after the Hindi
   assembly lock. Registry rename would be free (hash comes from config, not name)
   but is not planned.
